Privacy policy

Last updated: September 29, 2026

The short version: your reading stays on your device unless you choose otherwise. Breez works fully offline with no account. Some Pro features need the internet, and each one is described below, with exactly what it sends and to whom.

What stays on your device

The documents you open, your reading positions, settings, stats, goals and saved passages live on your device. Without an account, none of it leaves.

Anonymous usage statistics

Breez sends anonymous usage events through Aptabase, a privacy-focused analytics service: which screens and features are used and for how long, which paces people choose, how far readers get through setup. Events carry no names, accounts, device identifiers or reading content, and cannot be linked to you. You can turn this off in Settings, under Privacy.

If you sign in

An account is optional. You can sign in with Apple, with Google, or with your email and a one-time code, so your library, invites and Pro follow you between devices. There is no password. We keep the email address Apple or Google gives us (Apple lets you hide yours), or the one you type if you choose email sign-in, and an account id. Your email is used only to sign you in: the code is sent to it by our email service, and we never send marketing to it. Accounts are run on Supabase, hosted in the United States.

On the web, the Google button on the sign-in sheet is Google's own, so its script loads from Google (accounts.google.com) when you open the sign-in sheet or choose to add Google, and at no other time. It never loads while you read, and nothing about your reading goes to Google. In the iPhone app, Google sign-in uses Google's own sign-in screen. What Google does with a sign-in is covered by Google's privacy policy.

Pro features that use the internet

Sync and backup. Your library, positions, settings, stats, goals and saved passages are stored in your account so they appear on your other devices. Only you can read them.

Reading aids (AI). When you ask Breez to define a word, explain a passage, recap where you left off or summarise a chapter, the words involved (the word and its sentence, the passage, or the text before your place) are sent through our server to Google's Gemini API to write the answer. We use Google's paid service, so Google does not use this to train its models; it keeps requests for up to 55 days only to check for abuse. Nothing that identifies you is sent with them. We count how many aids you use, not what they said.

Natural voices. When you listen with a natural voice, the text being read is sent to our voice provider to make the audio, and the audio is stored in your account so it does not have to be made twice.

Browse. Opening Wikipedia, news or essays loads them from those sites, which see an ordinary request from your device, as a browser would.

Open in Breez from Safari and other apps. The page or text you send goes straight into Breez on your device.

Invites

If you invite friends, your account gets an invite code, and we record which friends joined from it and the free weeks each of you got. A friend who joins is shown to you by their first name, from their Apple or Google account, or as “A friend”; they are told this when they claim their week. Nothing else about them, and nothing about their reading, is ever shown.

To keep one free week per device, Breez sends a scrambled (hashed) version of a random id it made on your device, which our server scrambles again before keeping it. On iPhone it also uses Apple’s DeviceCheck, where Apple remembers two yes-or-no marks for the device and we keep nothing. Neither can be turned back into your device’s id, and neither is used for anything else.

Purchases

Apple handles payment; we never see your card. RevenueCat tells Breez whether your Pro plan is active, using an anonymous id.

What we never do

We do not sell your data, show ads, or track you across other apps and websites.

Deleting your data

Without an account, deleting the app deletes everything. With an account, open the You tab and choose Delete account: your account and everything stored in it are removed from our servers.

Children

Breez is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears here with a new date.
